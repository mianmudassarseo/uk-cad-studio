Architectural design Leicester

Architectural design and planning drawings in Leicester.

Extensions, loft conversions and HMO schemes across Leicester — a compact city with deep Victorian terraced stock and a strong multi-generational housing market.

Get a free project reviewCall +92 305 6205832

Leicester has a dense core of Victorian and Edwardian terraced housing surrounded by interwar and post-war suburban development, and a housing market shaped by two universities and one of the most established multi-generational living cultures in England.

That last point produces a particular pattern of work: larger rear and side extensions designed to accommodate extended families, annexe schemes, and reconfigurations that add ground floor bedrooms and bathrooms. These are design problems about circulation and privacy as much as floor area.

Leicester project types

Family extensions and annexes. A significant share of Leicester work involves creating additional living and sleeping space for extended family. The planning distinction between an ancillary annexe and a separate dwelling matters here, and we advise on it early because it determines the application route.

Terrace extensions and loft conversions across Clarendon Park, Stoneygate and Aylestone follow the familiar pattern, with rear dormers common on the two-storey terraces.

HMO conversions concentrate near the universities and in the inner suburbs. Leicester has used Article 4 directions in parts of the city to manage HMO concentration, so the planning route should be confirmed address by address.

Suburban semis in Evington, Knighton and Oadby generally have the plot width for two-storey side extensions, subject to the council's expectations about boundary gaps and street rhythm.

FAQs

Leicester planning questions

Local answers for property owners and developers in Leicester.

Do I need planning permission for a family annexe in Leicester?

It depends on whether the annexe is genuinely ancillary to the main house or functions as an independent dwelling. Accommodation used by a household member and sharing access and facilities may be acceptable as part of the existing dwelling; a self-contained unit with its own entrance, kitchen and bathroom will normally need full planning permission, often with a condition preventing separate sale.

What size extension can I build in Leicester without planning permission?

Standard permitted development allows single-storey rear extensions of three metres beyond the original rear wall on attached houses and four metres on detached, with a larger allowance available via prior approval. Height limits, boundary proximity and the 50 per cent curtilage rule all apply, and rights are restricted in conservation areas.

Are HMOs restricted in Leicester?

Article 4 directions restricting the permitted development right to create small HMOs apply in parts of Leicester, meaning full planning permission is required in those areas. Large HMOs always need permission. We check the current position for your specific address before advising.

Do you cover Leicestershire as well as the city?

Yes. We prepare drawings for projects in Oadby & Wigston, Blaby, Charnwood, Harborough, Hinckley & Bosworth and North West Leicestershire in addition to Leicester City Council.

How long do extension drawings take in Leicester?

A planning drawing set typically takes one to two weeks from survey, with the building regulations package taking a similar period afterwards. Larger annexe and multi-generational schemes usually need longer at the layout stage because the circulation has to be resolved carefully.
