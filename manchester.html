Architectural design Manchester

Architectural design and planning drawings in Manchester.

Extensions, loft conversions and HMO schemes across Manchester and the wider Greater Manchester boroughs — drawn for the city's Victorian terraces and its unusually active student rental market.

Manchester's residential stock is dominated by Victorian and Edwardian terraces and interwar semis, and its planning landscape is shaped by one thing more than any other: a rental market driven by three large universities and a rapidly growing professional population.

That means two very different kinds of client come to us here — homeowners extending a family house in Didsbury or Chorlton, and landlords and developers converting property in the corridor south of the city centre. The drawings each needs are different, and so is the planning route.

What Manchester projects usually involve

Rear and wrap-around extensions on Victorian terraces and 1930s semis are the most common homeowner project. Many fall within permitted development, but plots in the inner suburbs are often narrow with short gardens, so the allowance runs out faster than people expect and the design has to work harder for daylight.

Loft conversions, especially rear dormers, are common across the city's two-storey terraces. Manchester's older terraces frequently have generous ridge heights, which makes them good candidates — but the staircase run is usually the constraint on a narrow plan.

HMO conversions are a significant part of the local market, particularly in the southern corridor. Manchester has used Article 4 directions to remove permitted development rights for small HMO conversions in parts of the city, which means a full planning application where they apply — and applications are assessed against concentration of existing HMOs in the immediate area.

Local planning context

What shapes a Manchester application

The constraints we check before starting design work on any project in Greater Manchester.

Manchester City Council

The city's local plan and residential design guidance set the standards for extensions, conversions and amenity space. Neighbouring boroughs apply their own.

Article 4 HMO areas

Article 4 directions removing C3 to C4 permitted development rights apply in parts of Manchester, particularly around the southern student corridor. We confirm the current boundary for your address.

HMO licensing

Manchester operates mandatory licensing alongside selective and additional licensing in designated areas, each with its own amenity and room size standards.

Conservation areas

Areas including parts of Didsbury, Chorlton and Victoria Park have conservation status, restricting permitted development and controlling materials.

Greater Manchester boroughs

Trafford, Salford, Stockport, Tameside, Bury, Bolton, Oldham, Rochdale and Wigan each operate separate planning departments and policies.

Terraced plot constraints

Short rear gardens in inner suburbs frequently limit extension depth before the 50 per cent curtilage rule bites.

FAQs

Manchester planning questions

Local answers for property owners and developers in Manchester.

Do I need planning permission for an HMO in Manchester?

It depends on your address and the number of occupants. Converting to a small HMO for three to six people is permitted development nationally, but Article 4 directions in parts of Manchester remove that right and require a full planning application. Any HMO for seven or more occupants always needs permission. We check the current Article 4 boundary for your property before quoting.

Do you cover Greater Manchester as well as the city?

Yes. We work across Trafford, Salford, Stockport, Tameside, Bury, Bolton, Oldham, Rochdale and Wigan as well as Manchester City Council. Each authority applies its own local plan, so we check the relevant policy for the specific address.

What extension can I build on a Manchester terrace without planning permission?

Single-storey rear extensions on attached houses are typically limited to three metres beyond the original rear wall under standard permitted development, with a larger allowance available through the prior approval process. Height, boundary proximity and the 50 per cent curtilage rule all apply, and rights are restricted in conservation areas. We check your specific constraints first.

How long does Manchester City Council take to decide a planning application?

The statutory target is eight weeks for householder and minor applications. Validation can add time if the submitted drawings do not meet the council's requirements, which is the most common avoidable delay.

Can you prepare HMO licensing plans for Manchester landlords?

Yes. We produce layouts annotated with net room areas and amenity provision measured against the standards applying to your property, along with fire strategy drawings showing escape routes, compartmentation and alarm coverage.
