New build residential design

New build design, from plot feasibility to construction drawings.

Single dwellings, infill plots, garden-land sites and small residential schemes — designed around what the site can realistically support and what the local plan will actually accept.

Get a free project reviewCall +92 305 6205832

The difference between a plot and a building site is planning permission, and the difference between a permission and a profitable one is usually a few decisions made very early: how many units the site can carry, where the access goes, how the massing sits against the neighbours, and whether the daylight and overlooking distances stack up.

We work on single self-build dwellings, infill and backland plots, garden-land subdivisions and small residential schemes. On most of these projects the design work worth paying for happens before anything is drawn in detail — testing whether the site works at all, and at what density.

Feasibility first

Before concept design, we look at the constraints that decide the answer: the site's planning history and any previous refusals, the local plan allocation and policy position, flood zone and drainage, trees and any TPOs, protected species and ecology triggers, highways access and visibility splays, and the separation distances your council applies between habitable windows.

That work is cheap relative to the cost of designing a scheme that was never going to be supported. Where a site is marginal, a pre-application enquiry to the council is often the single best value spend on the whole project, and we will prepare the drawings for it.

Design and planning

Concept design covers site layout, orientation, massing and the relationship to neighbouring properties, followed by internal planning that meets space standards and works commercially. For a self-build the priority is usually how you want to live; for a developer it is usually gross internal area per pound of build cost. Both are legitimate briefs and they lead to different drawings.

The planning submission includes existing and proposed site plans, floor plans, elevations and sections, street scene elevations where the setting matters, a location plan, and the supporting drawn information a design and access statement needs. We coordinate with the consultants a site requires — ecologist, arboriculturist, drainage engineer, transport consultant — so the drawings and the reports agree with each other.

Technical and construction stage

Once permission is granted the drawings have to become buildable. That means a full building regulations package: construction plans and sections, foundation layout, drainage design, insulation and U-value schedules, ventilation strategy, thermal bridging and airtightness detail, accessibility under Part M and the fire strategy under Part B.

New dwellings also need a SAP assessment and an energy statement, and increasingly must respond to Part L requirements that materially affect the build-up thicknesses and glazing ratios. Those decisions belong in the drawings, not in a conversation with the site manager six months later.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to the questions we are asked most often.

Do I need an architect for a new build house?

You do not need a chartered architect by law — planning applications and building regulations submissions can be prepared by any competent designer. What you do need is someone who understands local planning policy and can produce a technically correct package. For unusual, listed or architecturally sensitive sites, a chartered architect is often worth the additional fee, and we will say so where that applies.

What drawings are needed for a new build planning application?

Typically an existing site plan, a proposed site or block plan, floor plans for each level, all elevations, sections, a location plan at 1:1250, and often a street scene elevation showing the proposal alongside neighbouring buildings. Most authorities also require a design and access statement for new dwellings.

How long does new build planning permission take?

Local authorities have a statutory target of eight weeks for minor applications, which covers most single dwellings and small schemes, though extensions of time are common. Larger schemes run to thirteen weeks. Allow additional time before submission for survey, design and any pre-application enquiry.

Can I build a house in my garden?

Sometimes. Garden-land and backland development is possible but depends heavily on your local plan, the size and shape of the plot, achieving safe vehicular access, separation distances to existing windows, tree constraints and the character of the street. It is exactly the kind of site where a feasibility review before design work is worth doing.

Do new builds need a SAP assessment?

Yes. New dwellings require SAP calculations to demonstrate compliance with Part L, along with an energy statement, and the results directly affect insulation thicknesses, glazing areas and heating specification. Because of that, energy compliance needs to be considered at design stage rather than bolted on afterwards.

Do you produce drawings for self-build projects?

Yes, and self-build is one of our more common project types. We can work to whatever level suits you — feasibility and planning only if you have a contractor lined up who will handle the technical side, or a complete package through to building regulations and construction information.
