Lawful Development Certificate: What It Is and When You Need One

Learn what a Lawful Development Certificate is, the difference between proposed and existing use, what evidence is needed and when to apply.

Planning14 September 2026By UK CAD Studio Editorial Team9 min read
Quick answer: A Lawful Development Certificate is a formal decision from the local planning authority confirming that an existing or proposed use or development is lawful for planning purposes. It is useful when you want evidence that work does not need a normal planning application.

Updated for 2026. This guide is written in simple terms for homeowners, landlords and residential developers. Planning rules quoted here relate mainly to England. Always check the exact property and local authority requirements before starting work.

Two main certificate types

GOV.UK guidance distinguishes certificates for existing development or use and certificates for proposed development or use. A proposed certificate is common where a homeowner wants to confirm that an extension or loft conversion is permitted development before building.

An existing certificate is evidence-based and can be used where work or a use has already happened and you need a formal planning-status decision.

What the council assesses

The question is lawfulness, not whether the council likes the design. For a proposed extension under permitted development, the authority checks the proposal against the legal limitations and conditions that apply.

That means accurate dimensions and a clear description are essential. If the drawings are vague, the certificate can only be as precise as the information submitted.

If you need project-specific drawings, see our house extension drawings. We also support residential projects in Manchester and Nottingham.

Evidence and drawings

A proposed application normally benefits from existing and proposed plans, elevations, roof information and a site plan. For existing uses or works, dated evidence may also be important.

The final certificate is tied to the specific facts and drawings considered. If the project later changes, the certificate may no longer cover it.

What a certificate does not do

A Lawful Development Certificate does not replace Building Regulations, listed building consent, licensing, party wall procedures or other legal requirements. It only addresses planning lawfulness.

This distinction is important because a project can be lawful in planning terms but still need technical approval.

When it is worth getting one

It is particularly useful before a permitted-development build, before a property sale or where there is uncertainty in the planning history.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Lawful Development Certificate the same as planning permission?

No. It confirms lawfulness; it is not a discretionary grant of planning permission.

Is an LDC compulsory for permitted development?

Not always, but it is useful formal evidence.

Can an LDC be refused?

Yes, if the evidence does not demonstrate that the development or use is lawful.

Does an LDC cover Building Regulations?

No. Building Regulations are a separate system.
