Measured Building Survey: What It Includes and What It Costs

Every extension, conversion and lease plan is drawn on top of a drawing of what already exists. Here is what a measured survey gives you, what it costs in the UK, and how to brief one properly.

Design & drawings Updated 19 October 2026 11 min read
Quick answer

A measured building survey records the existing size and layout of a building accurately on site and turns it into scaled CAD drawings: floor plans, and where needed elevations, sections and roof plans. It is the base for planning applications, building regulations drawings, lease plans and refurbishment design. Published UK prices for a simple floor plan survey of a flat or small house start at around £1,000 to £1,200 plus VAT, with larger houses, full elevation and section packages, or 3D laser scanning costing more. It is different from a RICS Level 2 or Level 3 building survey, which reports on condition rather than measurements.

Every drawing of a proposed extension, conversion or lease plan is drawn on top of a drawing of what already exists. If that existing drawing is wrong, everything built on it inherits the error: a room that fails HMO size standards by 30 mm, a steel beam ordered too short, or a lease plan rejected by HM Land Registry. A measured building survey is how you avoid that.

This guide explains what a measured survey includes, how it differs from other surveys, how much a measured survey costs in the UK, and how to brief one so you get drawings you can actually use.

Key takeaways

  • A measured survey produces accurate existing drawings. It does not assess the building's condition.
  • Typical outputs are floor plans, elevations, sections and roof plans in CAD and PDF.
  • Simple floor plan surveys start at around £1,000 to £1,200 plus VAT; more deliverables and bigger buildings cost more.
  • Agree the deliverables, scale, accuracy and file format in writing before the survey.
  • Estate agent floor plans are not measured surveys and should never be used for planning or lease plans.
  • Good access on survey day, including the loft and garden, saves cost and return visits.

What is a measured building survey?

A measured building survey (also called a measured survey, building measurement survey or existing drawings survey) is an accurate record of a building's dimensions taken on site with laser measuring tools, total stations or 3D laser scanners. The data is drawn up in CAD as scaled drawings. It is the starting point for almost every architectural project on an existing building.

Not every project needs every drawing. A lease plan for a flat may only need a floor plan. A loft conversion needs sections showing the roof and ridge height. A two storey extension needs elevations of the affected sides.

How much does a measured building survey cost?

Measured survey prices depend on the size and complexity of the building and what you need drawn. Published UK figures give a guide:

  • A simple flat with good access, floor plan only, has been quoted at around £1,000 plus VAT, typically 3 to 4 hours on site and a day of CAD work.
  • Specialist providers advertise measured building surveys from about £1,199 plus VAT for most residential properties in 2026.
  • Larger houses, multiple floors, full sets of elevations and sections, ornate buildings, or laser scanning all increase the price.

What affects the cost

  • Deliverables: floor plans only cost less than a full package with elevations, sections and roof plans.
  • Size and number of floors: each floor adds site and drawing time.
  • Level of detail: fixed fittings, ceiling features, services and joist directions all add time.
  • Accuracy and scale: tighter tolerances and larger scales need more measurement.
  • Access: high elevations, restricted rooms or roofs may need scanners, drones or return visits.
  • Output format: a full 3D or BIM model costs more than 2D CAD drawings.
  • Travel and turnaround: distance and urgent deadlines can add a premium.

Estate agent floor plans are drawn to sell, not to build from. They are often approximate, lack a stated scale and miss wall thicknesses. Using one as the base for planning drawings or a lease plan is one of the most common causes of refused or returned applications.

How a measured survey is carried out

  1. Brief: agree the drawings needed, scale, accuracy and file format.
  2. Site visit: the surveyor measures every room, opening and level using laser distance meters, a total station or a 3D scanner. A typical house takes a few hours.
  3. Drawing up: measurements are processed and drawn in CAD to a consistent layer and line standard.
  4. Checking: dimensions are cross-checked, for example overall lengths against the sum of rooms.
  5. Issue: PDF and DWG files, ready for the designer to draw the proposals on top.

How to brief a measured survey

A clear brief avoids paying twice. Include:

  • The purpose (planning, lease plan, building regulations, refurbishment).
  • Which drawings are needed and at what scale.
  • Whether ceiling heights, floor levels, joist directions or services should be recorded.
  • The required accuracy, often by reference to RICS guidance on measured surveys.
  • The file format (DWG version, PDF, Revit) and layer standard.
  • Access arrangements, including the loft, roof, garden and any locked rooms.

On survey day, clear access to walls and windows, the loft hatch and the garden boundaries. Having keys for outbuildings and any existing drawings to hand saves time.

Frequently asked questions

What is a measured building survey?

It is an accurate on-site survey of a building's dimensions, drawn up as scaled CAD drawings such as floor plans, elevations, sections and roof plans. It is used as the base for planning, building regulations and lease plan drawings.

How much does a measured survey cost?

Published UK prices for a simple floor plan survey of a flat or small house start at around £1,000 to £1,200 plus VAT. Larger houses, elevations and sections, or 3D laser scanning cost more.

What is the difference between a measured survey and a building survey?

A measured survey records dimensions and produces drawings. A RICS Level 2 or Level 3 building survey reports on the condition of a property and its defects, usually when buying.

How long does a measured survey take?

A typical house takes a few hours on site, and the drawings are usually issued within a few working days, depending on size and the deliverables needed.

Can I use an estate agent floor plan instead of a measured survey?

No. Estate agent plans are approximate marketing drawings without a reliable scale. They are not accurate enough for planning applications, building regulations or Land Registry lease plans.

Do I need a measured survey for planning permission?

You need accurate existing drawings, and a measured survey is the most reliable way to get them. Councils compare existing and proposed drawings, so errors in the existing plans can undermine the application.
