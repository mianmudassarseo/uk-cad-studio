Wrap Around Extension: Cost, Planning and Design Guide for 2026

A wrap around extension fills the side return and the back of a period house in one L shaped space. Here is what it costs in 2026, how planning works, and the design choices that matter.

House extensions Updated 9 October 2026 13 min read
Quick answer

A wrap around extension is an L shaped extension that fills the side return and continues across the back of the house, typically on a Victorian or Edwardian terrace. Published 2026 UK guides put the build cost at around £2,800 to £3,500 per m², so a mid-size 20 to 25 m² wrap around usually costs £72,000 to £105,000, with London around a quarter higher. Most wrap around extensions need planning permission, because the side element takes the design outside permitted development, and nearly all involve the Party Wall Act and steel beams designed by a structural engineer.

On a period terrace, the side return is that narrow, gloomy strip of garden beside the back addition. A wrap around extension takes in that strip and the space behind the back addition at the same time, turning a dark galley kitchen and separate dining room into one large, bright family space. It is one of the most effective extensions you can build on a terrace, and also one of the more technical ones.

This guide covers wrap around extension costs for 2026, planning permission, party walls and building regulations, and the layout decisions that make the space work. It covers England.

Key takeaways

  • A wrap around combines a side return extension and a rear extension into one L shaped space.
  • Typical 2026 costs run at £2,800 to £3,500 per m², higher than a simple rear extension because of steelwork and glazing.
  • Most wrap arounds need a householder planning application rather than permitted development.
  • The Party Wall Act almost always applies, and a build over agreement may be needed for shared drains.
  • Rooflights along the side return are what stop the middle of the house going dark.
  • Budget for structural engineering, party wall surveyors and building control on top of the build cost.

What is a wrap around extension?

A wrap around extension is a single storey extension that wraps around two sides of the house: along the side (usually the side return of a terrace or semi) and across the rear. It forms an L shape in plan. On a Victorian terrace it typically replaces the side return and extends a few metres into the garden, joining the kitchen in the back addition with the old dining room.

How much does a wrap around extension cost in 2026?

Published 2026 cost guides give these typical all-in ranges, excluding the kitchen units:

Medium, 20 to 25 m²£72,000 to £105,000£2,880 to £4,200
Large, 30 to 40 m²£90,000 to £130,000£2,600 to £3,800

London prices typically run around 22 to 28% above the national average, so a 25 m² wrap around in London is commonly quoted at £95,000 to £125,000. Smaller extensions cost more per m² because fixed costs such as steelwork, glazing and the kitchen connection are spread over less floor area.

What drives the cost

  • Steelwork. Removing the back addition's side wall and the rear wall usually needs steel beams. A single beam is commonly £2,800 to £5,500 fitted, and a goalpost frame of three steels £6,500 to £11,000 including engineer's fees.
  • Glazing. Bifold or sliding doors and rooflights are a large share of the budget. Large frameless sliders or a fully glazed roof strip can add tens of thousands.
  • Party wall costs. A surveyor is often around £1,000 to £2,500 per neighbour where an award is needed.
  • Drainage. Diverting a shared drain or building over a public sewer adds cost and a build over agreement with the water company.
  • Finishes. Underfloor heating, polished concrete or large format tiles, and the kitchen itself.

Do you need planning permission for a wrap around extension?

In most cases, yes. Permitted development allows single storey rear extensions up to 3 m on attached houses and side extensions no wider than half the original house. A wrap around is assessed as one extension, so it has to meet the rules for both a side extension and a rear extension at the same time. The combined L shape, particularly where it extends beyond the side wall of the back addition and then across the rear, usually breaks one of the limits. In conservation areas, side extensions are not permitted development at all.

A householder application is normally straightforward for a well-designed single storey wrap around. Officers focus on:

  • Height on the boundary. Keeping the boundary wall low, often around 2.5 to 3 m, protects the neighbour's light.
  • Depth beyond the rear wall, and the effect on the neighbour's windows, often tested with a 45 degree rule.
  • Roof form. A sloping glazed roof along the side return, or a flat roof with rooflights, both work if the boundary height is controlled.
  • Garden space left. Many councils expect a reasonable amount of garden to remain.

Party walls and build over agreements

A wrap around on a terrace or semi is built on or near the boundary, so the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 almost always applies. You must serve notice on the neighbours, typically at least one or two months before work starts depending on the type of work, and if they do not consent a party wall surveyor prepares an award. Starting without notice can lead to an injunction.

Terraces often share a combined drain running behind the houses. If the extension sits over or within 3 m of a public sewer, you need a build over agreement from the water company before work starts.

Building regulations for a wrap around extension

  • Structure (Part A): steel beams, padstones and foundations designed by a structural engineer, especially where the back addition walls are removed.
  • Energy (Part L): insulated floors, walls and roofs. Glazing above about 25% of the new floor area (plus the area of any windows removed) normally needs a compensating calculation.
  • Ventilation (Part F): extract to the kitchen and background ventilation.
  • Drainage (Part H): new gullies, rainwater outlets on flat roofs, and sewer connections.
  • Fire and escape (Part B): the extension must not create an inner room without an alternative escape route.

Wrap around extension design ideas

Bring light into the middle of the house

The biggest risk is a bright new kitchen at the back and a dark room in the middle. Rooflights or a glazed roof strip along the line of the old side return wall solve this. Ridge or lantern rooflights over the old dining room are also popular.

Plan the kitchen around the steelwork

Decide where the island, sink and tall units go before the steel positions are fixed. A downstand beam over the island can look intentional if planned, and awkward if not.

Keep a utility and WC

A wrap around often creates a narrow strip at the side of the old back addition, which works well for a utility, pantry or downstairs WC.

Think about the garden connection

A full width sliding door across the rear, or a corner opening with no post, makes the most of the L shape. Level thresholds need careful detailing for weather and drainage.

How a wrap around project runs

  1. Measured survey of the ground floor, rear and boundaries. See measured building survey cost.
  2. Design and planning drawings, then a householder planning application, usually decided within 8 weeks.
  3. Party wall notices and a build over agreement if needed, in parallel with planning.
  4. Structural design and building regulations drawings for tendering and building control.
  5. Builder tenders on the same detailed drawings, then construction, typically several months on site.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a wrap around extension cost?

Published 2026 UK guides put wrap around extensions at around £2,800 to £3,500 per m². A medium 20 to 25 m² extension typically costs £72,000 to £105,000, with London around a quarter higher.

Do you need planning permission for a wrap around extension?

Usually yes. A wrap around has to meet both the side and rear extension limits of permitted development, and the combined L shape normally breaks one of them. Side extensions are never permitted development in conservation areas.

What is the difference between a side return and a wrap around extension?

A side return extension fills only the narrow strip beside the back addition. A wrap around fills the side return and also extends across the rear of the house in an L shape.

Do I need a party wall agreement for a wrap around extension?

Almost always on a terrace or semi. You must serve notice under the Party Wall etc. Act 1996, and if the neighbour does not consent, a surveyor prepares a party wall award before work starts.

How long does a wrap around extension take?

Allow a few months for design, planning (usually 8 weeks for a decision), party wall and building regulations, then several months on site depending on size and complexity.

Will a wrap around extension make the middle of the house dark?

It can, unless you add rooflights or a glazed roof strip along the old side return wall. Planning the light at design stage is the best way to keep the middle room bright.
