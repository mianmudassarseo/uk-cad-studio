Single-Storey Rear Extension Rules: Permitted Development Explained

Understand single-storey rear extension rules in England, including permitted development, larger home extensions, boundaries, height and certificates.

House extensions12 September 2026By UK CAD Studio Editorial Team9 min read
Quick answer: A single-storey rear extension can often be permitted development in England if it stays within the relevant depth, height, boundary and plot-coverage limits. Larger rear extensions may use a prior approval process rather than a standard householder application.

Updated for 2026. This guide is written in simple terms for homeowners, landlords and residential developers. Planning rules quoted here relate mainly to England. Always check the exact property and local authority requirements before starting work.

The normal rear-extension route

For many houses, a modest single-storey rear extension can fall within Class A permitted development. The detailed test considers the original rear wall, previous enlargements, eaves height, overall height, boundary distance and total site coverage.

The commonly quoted depth limits are only part of the test. A design that passes on depth can still fail on another condition.

Larger home extension prior approval

The larger home extension route can allow deeper single-storey rear extensions through a prior approval process involving neighbour consultation. It is not automatic permission and it has its own procedure and conditions.

If you want to use this route, the drawings should clearly show the existing and proposed dimensions so the local authority can assess the notification.

If you need project-specific drawings, see our house extension drawings. We also support residential projects in Newcastle upon Tyne and Bristol.

Height and boundary checks

Where an extension sits close to a boundary, the permitted eaves height becomes especially important. The overall extension height and roof design also need to stay within the relevant limits.

These checks should be made on elevations and sections, not just a floor plan.

When permitted development does not apply

Flats, some converted houses, properties with removed rights and certain designated locations need a different planning approach. Previous extensions can also use up part of the allowance.

If there is uncertainty, a full planning application or a formal Lawful Development Certificate route may be safer than relying on an informal opinion.

Frequently asked questions

How far can a rear extension project under permitted development?

Common national limits depend on whether the house is detached or attached and whether the normal or larger-home-extension route applies. Always check the full current rules.

Does a 3m rear extension need planning permission?

Not necessarily. Many 3m rear extensions can be permitted development, but property-specific restrictions still apply.

Can I build a flat roof rear extension?

Often yes, provided the proposal meets the applicable planning and height rules.

Do I need a Lawful Development Certificate?

It is not always mandatory, but it gives formal evidence that the proposal is lawful for planning purposes.
