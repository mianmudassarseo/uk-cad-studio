Loft Conversion Cost UK 2026: What Should You Budget?

Loft conversion cost guide for 2026 covering Velux, dormer, hip-to-gable and mansard projects, plus design, structural and approval costs.

Loft conversions2 September 2026By UK CAD Studio Editorial Team9 min read
Quick answer: A typical UK loft conversion in 2026 can range from roughly £27,500 for a straightforward rooflight conversion to £75,000 or more for larger or more complex schemes. Dormer conversions are commonly around the middle of that range, but location, structure and finish can change the final cost significantly.

Updated for 2026. This guide is written in simple terms for homeowners, landlords and residential developers. Planning rules quoted here relate mainly to England. Always check the exact property and local authority requirements before starting work.

Typical 2026 loft conversion costs

Published UK cost guides put simple rooflight or Velux-style conversions at the lower end, with dormer, hip-to-gable and mansard schemes costing more because they require greater structural change. For a typical project, a broad budget of about £27,500 to £75,000 is a useful starting range, not a quotation.

The right comparison is not simply price per square metre. Roof geometry, headroom, steelwork, staircase position, fire-safety work and the amount of new roof construction often matter more than floor area alone.

Cost by conversion type

A rooflight conversion is usually the least disruptive because the main roof shape stays largely intact. A dormer adds useful full-height floor area. Hip-to-gable work can unlock space in semi-detached and end-terrace houses, while a mansard can create the most space but usually involves extensive roof reconstruction.

Before choosing a type, check whether the existing roof has enough usable height and whether the new stair can arrive safely without sacrificing too much of the floor below.

Professional and approval costs

Your budget should allow for measured survey work, design drawings, structural calculations, Building Regulations, and planning or a Lawful Development Certificate where relevant. These costs are small compared with construction but they influence whether builders can price the job accurately.

A cheaper drawing package that leaves key technical decisions unresolved can make tender prices less reliable and lead to more changes during construction.

What pushes the cost up

Common cost drivers include moving water tanks or services, replacing the roof covering, complex steelwork, new bathrooms, bespoke stairs, party wall matters, difficult scaffold access and high-spec finishes. London and other high-cost areas can also be more expensive than regional averages.

Keep a contingency for hidden conditions. Roof timbers, chimney structure and historic alterations are not always fully visible until work starts.

How to control the budget

Start with a clear brief, measured drawings and a conversion type that suits the roof. Freeze the layout before detailed structural design, and ask builders to price the same information rather than different assumptions.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a dormer loft conversion in 2026?

A broad UK guide is around £40,000 to £60,000 for many 30m² dormer projects, but the exact price depends on structure, specification and location.

What is the cheapest loft conversion?

A rooflight or Velux-style conversion is usually the cheapest where the existing roof already has suitable height and volume.

Do loft conversions need planning permission?

Many fall under permitted development, but not all. Planning permission may be needed where limits are exceeded or rights are restricted.

Do I need Building Regulations drawings?

Yes, loft conversions normally require Building Regulations compliance because they affect structure, stairs, fire safety, insulation and ventilation.
