Building Regulations for a House Extension: Homeowner Guide

A practical guide to Building Regulations for house extensions, covering structure, insulation, fire safety, ventilation, drainage and technical drawings.

Building regulations11 September 2026By UK CAD Studio Editorial Team9 min read
Quick answer: Most house extensions need Building Regulations approval even when planning permission is not required. The technical design must address structure, fire safety, thermal performance, ventilation, drainage, electrical work and other relevant standards.

Updated for 2026. This guide is written in simple terms for homeowners, landlords and residential developers. Planning rules quoted here relate mainly to England. Always check the exact property and local authority requirements before starting work.

Why Building Regulations apply

An extension creates new foundations, walls, roof, openings and services. Building Regulations are there to ensure those elements meet national technical standards.

The approval is separate from planning, so an extension under permitted development can still require a full technical package.

Structure and foundations

The design needs suitable foundations for ground conditions and loads, safe support where existing walls are opened up, and a stable roof. Structural calculations are commonly needed for steels or significant alterations.

Technical drawings should show where structural information interfaces with the architectural layout so the builder is not forced to guess.

If you need project-specific drawings, see our building regulations drawings. We also support residential projects in Southampton and Leeds.

Energy, ventilation and moisture

New floors, walls, roofs, windows and doors need to meet thermal standards. Ventilation must suit the new rooms and any enlarged kitchen or bathroom. Damp protection and roof ventilation also need to be coordinated.

Good detailing avoids common problems such as cold bridges, condensation and awkward service routes.

Fire, access and drainage

Depending on the project, the design may need fire doors, escape windows, smoke detection or protected routes. Drainage changes must work with existing systems, and steps or level changes should be considered early.

The requirements vary with the layout, which is why generic notes are not a substitute for project-specific drawings.

What a drawing package should include

A useful package normally includes plans, sections, elevations, construction build-ups, key dimensions and notes tied to the project. Structural engineer information is then coordinated where required.

Frequently asked questions

Do all extensions need Building Regulations?

Most do, although limited exemptions exist for certain small structures such as some conservatories and porches where specific conditions are met.

Do Building Regulations drawings include structural calculations?

Structural calculations are usually prepared by a structural engineer and coordinated with the architectural technical package.

Can a builder start before approval?

The correct procedure depends on the approval route, but starting without adequate information increases risk. Check the chosen building-control process before work begins.

Are Building Regulations the same across the UK?

No. This article focuses mainly on England; Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have different systems and requirements.
