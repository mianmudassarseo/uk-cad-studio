House Extension Cost UK 2026: A Practical Budget Guide

House extension cost guide for 2026, including cost per m², single and two-storey projects, professional fees, approvals and budget risks.

House extensions4 September 2026By UK CAD Studio Editorial Team9 min read
Quick answer: A useful 2026 UK guide for a standard house extension is roughly £1,800 to £3,000 per square metre for construction, before some professional fees, specialist items and high-end finishes. Complex projects and expensive locations can sit above that range.

Updated for 2026. This guide is written in simple terms for homeowners, landlords and residential developers. Planning rules quoted here relate mainly to England. Always check the exact property and local authority requirements before starting work.

2026 extension cost per square metre

Current UK cost guides commonly place a standard finished house extension around £1,800 to £3,000 per m², with shell-only construction lower. This is a budgeting range rather than a contractor quote.

A 20m² extension at £2,400 per m² would suggest about £48,000 for the main build before you add items such as professional fees, structural design, planning, building control, kitchens, bathrooms, party wall costs or unusual ground conditions.

Single versus two-storey costs

A two-storey extension costs more overall but can give better value per square metre because the same foundations and roof support more floor area. The structural design, access and effect on neighbours also become more important.

Do not compare a rear kitchen extension with a simple shell extension on headline price alone. The fit-out can be a large part of the final spend.

Costs people forget

Common omissions include VAT, structural engineer fees, drainage changes, utility moves, building control, planning fees, party wall matters, temporary works, landscaping and making good the existing house.

A realistic budget should separate construction cost, professional fees, statutory fees, fixtures and a contingency. That makes it easier to see where savings are possible without weakening the design.

Why drawings affect price certainty

Builders can price more accurately when they receive coordinated drawings and a clear specification. If the quote is based only on a sketch, each contractor may assume different insulation, structure, drainage and finishes.

Planning drawings define the form of the project. Building Regulations drawings then provide the technical information needed to develop a buildable package.

How to keep an extension on budget

Set the maximum budget early, avoid unnecessary structural complexity, keep wet services near existing drainage where practical and make decisions before work starts. Compare like-for-like quotes and ask what is excluded.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 20m² extension cost in the UK?

Using a broad 2026 construction range of £1,800–£3,000 per m², the main build could be about £36,000–£60,000 before project-specific extras.

Is a two-storey extension cheaper per m²?

It can be better value per m² because foundations and roof costs are shared across two floors, although the total project cost is higher.

Are planning and drawings included in builder prices?

Not always. Professional fees and statutory fees are often separate, so check each quote carefully.

How much contingency should I keep?

The right amount depends on project risk, but it is sensible to keep a dedicated contingency rather than spend the full budget on the initial contract sum.
