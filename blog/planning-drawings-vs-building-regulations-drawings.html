Planning Drawings vs Building Regulations Drawings: What Do You Need?

Compare planning drawings and Building Regulations drawings, what each package contains, when you need them and why one cannot normally replace the other.

Design & drawings20 September 2026By UK CAD Studio Editorial Team9 min read
Quick answer: Planning drawings show what you want to build and how it relates to the site. Building Regulations drawings explain how the work will meet technical standards. Planning drawings are usually simpler; technical drawings contain construction build-ups, compliance notes and coordination with structural information.

Updated for 2026. This guide is written in simple terms for homeowners, landlords and residential developers. Planning rules quoted here relate mainly to England. Always check the exact property and local authority requirements before starting work.

What planning drawings are for

Planning officers need to understand scale, appearance, position and impact. The drawing set therefore focuses on floor plans, elevations, roof form and site context.

The set should be accurate and coordinated, but it does not need to specify every insulation layer, fixing or structural detail.

What Building Regulations drawings are for

Building control and contractors need technical information. This can include wall and roof build-ups, thermal performance, ventilation, drainage, fire safety, dimensions and references to structural calculations.

The drawings are closer to how the project is actually built.

If you need project-specific drawings, see our building regulations drawings. We also support residential projects in Liverpool and London.

Why one set cannot simply replace the other

A planning drawing can be approved while leaving many construction choices open. If a builder works from it alone, those decisions are made informally on site, which can create cost and compliance risk.

The technical stage turns the approved shape into a coordinated construction solution.

When to order each package

For projects needing planning permission, the usual sequence is planning first and technical design after the main form is approved. For straightforward permitted-development schemes, the stages can sometimes overlap.

If the layout may change during planning, delaying some technical work can avoid paying to redesign it.

A coordinated service

Using the same measured survey and CAD base across both stages reduces drawing conflicts and keeps revisions traceable.

Frequently asked questions

Can planning drawings be used for Building Control?

They are usually not detailed enough on their own.

Do Building Regulations drawings guarantee planning approval?

No. Planning and Building Regulations are separate assessments.

Which drawings do builders need?

Builders benefit from the technical package, structural information and specification rather than planning drawings alone.

Can I use different designers for each stage?

Yes, but the second designer should receive the approved drawings and accurate survey information so the packages stay coordinated.
