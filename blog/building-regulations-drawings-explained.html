Building Regulations Drawings Explained: What's Included, Examples and Approval

What goes into a building regulations drawing package, a worked example for a rear extension, which Approved Documents apply, and how full plans, building notices and completion certificates work in England.

Building regulations Updated 28 October 2026 Published 8 April 2026 15 min read
Quick answer

Building regulations drawings (also called building control drawings or building regs plans) are the technical drawings that show how your extension, loft conversion or alteration will meet the Building Regulations. A typical package includes dimensioned floor plans, construction sections, foundation and roof details, insulation and U-value notes, fire safety, ventilation and drainage layouts, and references to the structural engineer's calculations. In England you submit them through a full plans application, which gets a decision within five weeks (or two months by agreement), or use a building notice for some smaller domestic work.

Planning permission decides whether you can build something. Building regulations decide whether what you build is safe, warm, dry, ventilated and structurally sound. The drawings for the second question are a different set from the first, and they are also the set your builder prices from. Vague technical drawings produce vague quotes, and vague quotes produce variations on site.

This guide covers England. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland run their own building standards systems.

Key takeaways

  • Building regulations approval is separate from planning. Permitted development work still needs it.
  • A good package covers every Approved Document that applies, not just floor plans and a section.
  • Full plans gives you an approved design before work starts. A building notice lets you start sooner but gives no advance approval.
  • Structural calculations usually come from a separate engineer and must be coordinated with the drawings.
  • Building control is carried out by the council or a registered building control approver, never by the person who drew the plans.
  • The completion certificate at the end is the document buyers' solicitors will ask for.

What are building regulations drawings?

Building regulations drawings describe how the walls, floors, roof, foundations, stairs, openings and services of a project will be built so that they comply with the Building Regulations 2010. People call them by several names: building control drawings, building regulation drawings, building regs drawings, building regs plans, technical drawings, construction drawings or working drawings. They all mean broadly the same package.

They do two jobs. First, they let the building control body check compliance before and during construction. Second, they give every builder the same detailed information, so three quotes from three contractors can actually be compared.

Full plans vs building notice: which route should you use?

Not available forAvailable for all workBuildings covered by the Fire Safety Order, such as shops, offices and the common parts of flats, and certain work near public sewers

For most extensions and loft conversions we recommend full plans. You get a checked design before any money is spent on site, and every builder prices exactly the same scope. Under the 2023 reforms, a building control approval in England also lapses if work has not started within three years, so do not apply too far ahead.

If work has already been done without approval, a regularisation certificate may be possible for unauthorised work carried out on or after 11 November 1985. It often means opening up finished work for inspection, so it is no substitute for getting approval at the time.

How to get building regulations approval

  1. Choose a building control body. Either your local authority or a registered building control approver (the role formerly called an approved inspector).
  2. Prepare the drawings and calculations. The architectural package plus the structural engineer's calculations.
  3. Submit and pay the fee. Building control charges are set by each council or approver, not nationally, so get a quote.
  4. Respond to comments. It is normal for the assessor to ask questions or request small revisions before approval.
  5. Book inspections. Typically at foundations, damp course, drainage, structural steel and completion.
  6. Get the completion certificate. Keep it with your property documents for when you sell.

The official process is set out in GOV.UK guidance on building regulations approval.

Planning drawings vs building regulations drawings

Planning drawings show what the development will look like and how it sits on the site. Building regulations drawings show how it will be built. A planning set can be approved while leaving most construction decisions open, which is why it should never be treated as a construction package. Our full comparison of planning drawings and building regulations drawings walks through the differences, and planning permission vs building regulations explains the two approvals.

Who prepares and checks the drawings?

Architects, architectural technologists, designers and CAD technicians can prepare building regulations drawings within their competence. Structural calculations need a structural engineer, and specialist fire or energy assessments may need other consultants. Since October 2023, the Building Safety Act dutyholder regime also expects clients to appoint designers and builders who are competent for the work.

The checking is done by the building control body: your local authority or a registered building control approver. A drawing provider can prepare a compliant submission, but nobody can honestly promise approval without any comments.

How much do building regulations drawings cost?

Fees depend mainly on these factors, and you should always get an itemised quote that separates them:

  • Whether accurate existing drawings exist or a measured survey is needed.
  • The size and complexity of the project and the number of storeys.
  • Structural changes, which bring in an engineer's calculations as a separate fee.
  • Whether planning drawings already exist and can be developed, or the design starts from scratch.
  • Specialist inputs such as SAP calculations, fire strategy or acoustic design.
  • The building control fee itself, which is charged separately by the council or approver.

For typical ranges across drawing stages, see our architectural drawings cost guide for 2026. It usually makes sense to prepare the technical drawings once planning is approved or the scheme is fixed, but before you ask builders for prices.

Why the completion certificate matters

Once the final inspection is passed, the building control body issues a completion certificate (sometimes called a final certificate). It is evidence that the work was inspected and found to comply. Buyers' solicitors routinely ask for it, and missing certificates are one of the most common causes of delay in a sale. If you have lost one, the council that inspected the work can usually supply a copy.

Frequently asked questions

What are building regulations drawings?

They are technical drawings, also called building control drawings or building regs plans, that show how a project will meet the Building Regulations. They cover structure, fire safety, insulation, ventilation, drainage and stairs, and are used for building control approval and for builders' pricing.

Do I need building regulations drawings for a house extension?

Almost always. Extensions need building regulations approval even when planning permission is not required, and a full plans application needs a complete drawing package. A building notice avoids drawings at submission, but building control can still ask for them.

Can I use planning drawings for building control?

Only as a starting point. Planning drawings show appearance and layout but lack construction sections, specifications, structural references and drainage details, so they need developing into a technical package.

What is the difference between a full plans application and a building notice?

A full plans application submits detailed drawings for approval, with a decision within five weeks or two months by agreement. A building notice lets work start two days after submission with no advance approval, and compliance is checked on site.

How long does building regulations approval take?

A full plans application must be decided within five weeks, or up to two months if you agree to an extension. A building notice has no approval stage, so work can begin two days after it is submitted.

Are structural calculations included in building regulations drawings?

Usually not. Calculations are prepared by a structural engineer as a separate service and submitted with the drawings. Check whether your quote includes them or only coordinates with them.

What is a regularisation certificate?

It is a certificate a local authority can issue for building work carried out without approval on or after 11 November 1985, once it has inspected the work and any required changes have been made.

Do these rules apply across the UK?

No. This guide covers England. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own building standards, procedures and guidance.
