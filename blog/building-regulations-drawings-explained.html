Building Regulations Drawings Explained: What You Need in England

A practical guide to construction drawings, Approved Documents, full plans applications and building notices for extensions and home alterations in England.

Building regulations Updated 12 October 2026 10 min read

Building regulations drawings show how proposed construction work is intended to meet relevant technical requirements. For a house extension, loft conversion or other residential alteration, a coordinated package may include detailed plans, sections, construction notes, insulation specifications, drainage and ventilation information.

This guide covers England. Building standards and approval processes differ in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Requirements also depend on the type and age of the property, the scope of work and the applicable transitional arrangements.

Quick answer: Planning permission addresses whether development is acceptable in planning terms. Building regulations deal with the technical standards of building work. Having planning permission does not automatically mean the construction details meet building regulations.

What are building regulations drawings?

Building regulations drawings, sometimes called building control drawings or construction drawings, describe how walls, floors, roofs, stairs, openings and other elements will be built. They help the building control body review compliance and give builders more consistent information for estimating and carrying out the works.

Not every project needs the same level of detail. A straightforward alteration may need fewer drawings than a two-storey extension, loft conversion or HMO conversion. Structural calculations and specialist designs may also be required and should be prepared by appropriately competent specialists.

What is included in a building regulations drawing package?

  • Dimensioned floor plans: proposed layouts, openings, room uses, key dimensions and relevant notes.
  • Sections and construction details: floor, wall, roof and foundation arrangements, insulation, damp-proofing, junctions and weatherproofing.
  • Elevations and roof information: external changes, levels, openings and roof arrangements where relevant.
  • Fire safety information: means of escape, detection, fire separation and protected routes where applicable.
  • Ventilation and drainage details: extraction, fresh-air provision, pipe routes and connection information appropriate to the project.
  • Thermal performance information: proposed insulation and energy-related specifications, with calculations when required.
  • Construction notes and schedules: relevant product, material and workmanship specifications.
  • Structural information: beam, lintel and foundation details supported by engineering design and calculations when necessary.

The exact package should be agreed before work begins. For details of our remote drawing preparation service, see building regulations drawings for UK residential projects.

Which Approved Documents apply?

In England, the government publishes Approved Documents giving practical guidance on ways to meet building regulations. The relevant documents depend on the project. Common examples include:

  • Part A, Structure: stability, foundations, beams and load-bearing elements.
  • Part B, Fire safety: escape provisions, alarms and fire-resisting construction.
  • Part C, Site preparation and moisture: damp resistance, ground conditions and moisture protection.
  • Part F, Ventilation: adequate ventilation and extraction.
  • Part G, Sanitation and hot water safety: sanitary facilities and water-related requirements.
  • Part H, Drainage and waste: foul and surface-water drainage.
  • Part K, Protection from falling: stairs, guards and related safety provisions.
  • Part L, Conservation of fuel and power: thermal and energy performance.
  • Part M, Access: applicable access and use requirements.
  • Part P, Electrical safety: domestic electrical safety requirements.

Other requirements can apply, including Part E (sound), Part O (overheating) and Part S (electric vehicle charging infrastructure), especially for certain new dwellings or specified works. Not every Part applies to every extension. Check the current edition and commencement rules of the relevant guidance, rather than reusing a specification from an older project.

Full plans vs building notice: which route is suitable?

For many types of domestic building work in England, building control approval can follow a full plans application or a building notice route. However, building notices are not available for every type of work or building. Ask the relevant building control body which route applies before submitting.

Full plans application

Under the full plans route, drawings and other required information are submitted for assessment. The decision is usually due within five weeks, or up to two months with your agreement. Questions or revisions may be required. An approval does not remove the need for site inspections or compliant construction.

This approach can be particularly useful when an extension involves significant structural works, detailed construction information, or multiple contractors pricing the same scope.

Building notice

For eligible smaller projects, a building notice allows work to start two days after submission. It does not provide formal advance approval of plans. Building control may still request plans, calculations or other information and inspect the work as it proceeds. A building notice therefore does not mean construction drawings are never needed.

Important: Some work, including certain buildings subject to the Fire Safety Order, requires a full plans application. Check the correct process with your local authority building control team or registered building control approver.

For the official application routes and timescales, read GOV.UK guidance on building regulations approval.

Planning drawings vs building regulations drawings

Planning drawings generally communicate the proposed development's appearance, scale, layout and relationship to its surroundings. Building regulations drawings focus on how the proposed work meets relevant technical standards. A planning drawing set should not automatically be treated as a complete construction drawing package.

For example, if you are extending a property, start with our guide to planning drawings for a house extension, then review the dedicated house extension drawings service for the drawings you may need through planning and building control stages.

Who prepares and checks the drawings?

Architectural designers and CAD technicians can prepare coordinated drawings and details within their competence. Structural calculations, specialist fire strategies or other technical designs may require appropriately qualified professionals. Building control assessment and inspections are undertaken by the relevant building control body, not the drawing provider.

In England, building control may be provided by a local authority or a registered building control approver, subject to the rules for the project. The provider of drawings should not promise that every submission will be approved without comments.

How much do building regulations drawings cost, and when should you start?

The fee depends on property size, whether reliable measured drawings already exist, design complexity, structural changes and the information required for submission. Engineering calculations, surveys, application charges and specialist reports may be separate services, so ask for an itemised quotation.

It is usually practical to develop building regulations drawings once the scheme is sufficiently defined, before obtaining detailed contractor prices or starting construction. Some technical design can proceed alongside planning, but later design changes may create additional drawing work.

Why does a completion certificate matter?

Building control inspections and the relevant completion documentation help demonstrate that the completed works have been assessed through the required process. This paperwork may be requested during a future property sale. Retrospective regularisation may be possible for eligible historic work, but it can require opening up finished construction and is not a substitute for obtaining approval properly at the time.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need building regulations drawings for a house extension?

Many extensions need technical information showing how the work will meet building regulations. The exact drawings and calculations depend on your design and whether you use a full plans application or another permitted route.

Can I use planning permission drawings for building control?

Planning drawings can provide a starting point, but they often lack the construction details and technical specifications required for a full plans assessment.

Can building work start before full plans approval?

Depending on the applicable route and notice requirements, work may start before a full plans decision. Doing so carries the risk of needing changes if the plans or construction do not comply. Confirm your obligations with the building control body.

Are structural calculations included in architectural drawings?

Not necessarily. Engineering design and calculations are often a separate scope. Confirm what is included in your quote and who is responsible for structural design.

Do these rules apply across the whole UK?

No. This article explains the position in England. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own regulatory frameworks and procedures.
