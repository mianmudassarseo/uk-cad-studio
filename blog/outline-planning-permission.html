Outline Planning Permission: What It Is and How It Works

Outline planning permission settles whether a new home can be built on a site before you pay for the detailed design. Here is how it works, how long it lasts, and when full planning is the better choice.

Planning Updated 26 October 2026 11 min read
Quick answer

Outline planning permission establishes whether a development, usually new homes, is acceptable in principle before the detailed design is fixed. The details are left as reserved matters: access, appearance, landscaping, layout and scale. In England, you normally have 3 years from the outline approval to apply for the reserved matters, and must start building within 2 years of the last reserved matters approval. Outline permission is useful for testing a plot, selling land with planning, or securing finance, but you cannot build until the reserved matters are approved.

Designing a new house in full detail costs money. If the council then decides a house is not acceptable on the site at all, that money is wasted. Outline planning permission lets you settle the big question first, whether a home or a small scheme can be built there, and leave the detailed design for later.

This guide explains what outline planning permission covers, how it differs from full planning, how long it lasts, the drawings needed, and when an alternative such as permission in principle might suit you better. It covers England.

Key takeaways

  • Outline permission confirms the principle of development; reserved matters approval confirms the detail.
  • The five reserved matters are access, appearance, landscaping, layout and scale.
  • Reserved matters must normally be submitted within 3 years, and work must start within 2 years of the final approval.
  • Outline applications still need a location plan, a site plan and enough information to judge the scheme, often including indicative layouts.
  • It is popular for garden plots, backland sites and land being sold with planning.
  • Full planning permission is often quicker overall if you already know exactly what you want to build.

What is outline planning permission?

Outline planning permission is a type of planning permission granted under the Town and Country Planning (Development Management Procedure) (England) Order 2015. It approves the general principle of a development, such as "one detached dwelling" or "up to six homes", with some or all of the detailed matters reserved for later approval.

The five reserved matters
Reserved matterWhat it covers
AccessHow vehicles, cyclists and pedestrians get into and around the site
AppearanceMaterials, architectural style, windows and external finishes
LandscapingPlanting, boundary treatments, surfacing and trees
LayoutWhere buildings, roads and open spaces are positioned
ScaleHeight, width and length of each building

You choose which matters to reserve. Access is often decided at outline stage, because the council needs to know the site can be safely reached.

Outline vs full planning permission

A householder extension cannot use the outline route. Outline applications are for new buildings and larger developments.

When to apply for outline planning permission

  • Testing a garden plot or backland site before paying for a full design.
  • Selling land with planning, which can significantly increase its value and lets the buyer design their own home.
  • Securing finance or a purchase, where a lender or seller wants certainty on the principle.
  • Larger sites where the layout and house types will be refined later.

If you already know exactly what you want to build and plan to start soon, a full application is usually faster and avoids paying for two rounds. A pre-application enquiry with the council is often the best value first step either way.

How long does outline planning permission last?

Under section 92 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990, unless the council sets different limits:

  • An application for approval of reserved matters must be made within 3 years of the outline permission.
  • Development must begin within 2 years of the final reserved matters approval.

If reserved matters are not submitted in time, the outline permission lapses and you would need to apply again, possibly under different local policies.

What an outline application needs

Even though the detail is reserved, councils still need enough information to judge whether the principle works. A typical outline application for one or a few homes includes:

Typical outline application drawings and documents
ItemPurpose
Location plan at 1:1250Shows the site edged red in context
Site plan showing accessShows the access point and visibility, especially if access is not reserved
Indicative layoutShows how the homes, parking and gardens could fit on the site
Scale parametersMaximum and minimum height, width and length of buildings
Design and access statementNeeded for major development and in some designated areas
Supporting reportsSuch as a tree survey, flood risk assessment, ecology or highways statement, depending on the site

Councils can ask for more detail if they think the principle cannot be judged without it, for example on tight infill plots where overlooking and daylight are critical. Our guide to drawings needed for planning permission covers drawing standards, and a measured or topographical survey gives the indicative layout a reliable base.

Reserved matters applications

Once outline permission is granted, you or a buyer submit the reserved matters with full drawings: floor plans, elevations, a detailed site layout, materials and landscaping. The council assesses only the reserved matters, not the principle, which is already decided. Reserved matters must stay within the outline permission and its conditions. Major changes may need a new outline application.

Community Infrastructure Levy (CIL), where your council charges it, is normally calculated when the final details are approved. Self-builders may be able to claim an exemption, but it must be applied for and accepted before work starts. Missing that step can make the full levy payable.

How long does an outline application take?

Councils aim to decide minor applications, which include fewer than 10 homes, within 8 weeks, and major applications within 13 weeks. Reserved matters applications have their own decision period. See how long planning permission takes for what causes delays.

How much does outline planning permission cost?

Outline application fees are set nationally and calculated by site area for outline applications. Check the current fee with the Planning Portal fee calculator. On top of the fee, budget for an indicative design, any surveys and reports the site needs, and later the reserved matters application and its fee. For drawing costs, see our architectural drawings cost guide.

Outline permission vs permission in principle

Permission in principle is a separate two stage route for housing-led development. The first stage looks only at the location, land use and amount of development, with very little information. The second stage, technical details consent, deals with the design. Applications for permission in principle are available for minor housing-led schemes. It can be cheaper and quicker for testing a site, but gives less certainty than outline permission on matters such as access. A planning consultant or designer can advise which suits your site.

Frequently asked questions

What is outline planning permission?

It is planning permission that approves the principle of a development, usually new homes, while leaving details such as access, appearance, landscaping, layout and scale as reserved matters to be approved later.

How long does outline planning permission last?

Normally you must apply for reserved matters within 3 years of the outline approval, and start building within 2 years of the final reserved matters approval, unless the council sets different limits.

What are reserved matters?

Reserved matters are the details left out of an outline permission: access, appearance, landscaping, layout and scale. They must be approved in a later application before building starts.

Can I build with outline planning permission?

No. You must first get reserved matters approval for the details, and discharge any pre-commencement conditions, before construction can start.

Is outline or full planning permission better?

Outline is better for testing a site, selling land or when the design is not settled. Full permission is usually quicker and cheaper overall if you already know exactly what you want to build.

Does outline planning permission increase land value?

Usually yes. Land with planning permission for housing is typically worth considerably more than land without it, because the main planning risk has been removed for the buyer.
