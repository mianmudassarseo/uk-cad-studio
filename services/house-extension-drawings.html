House extension drawings

House extension drawings that get built, not just approved.

Single-storey, two-storey, side-return and wrap-around extension drawings — surveyed accurately, drawn to your local authority's submission standards and detailed properly for the people who have to build them.

An extension is usually the single largest change a household will make to its home, and almost all of the risk sits in the first few weeks — before anyone lifts a trowel. A drawing set that is vague about levels, wrong about the position of a boundary, or silent on how the new roof meets the old one is the reason projects stall at validation, get refused on design grounds, or come back from three builders with three wildly different prices.

We prepare complete extension drawing packages for homeowners, builders and developers across the UK: a measured survey of what is actually there, a proposed design that responds to how you want to live, and a technical set that a building control body and a contractor can both work from.

Extension types we draw

Single-storey rear extensions — the most common project we handle. The design work is usually about daylight: how far you can go back before the middle of the plan turns dark, and whether rooflights, a glazed link or a raised ceiling solve it.

Side-return extensions — filling in the narrow alley beside a Victorian or Edwardian terrace. Small in footprint, disproportionately large in effect, and technically fiddly where the new structure meets the existing flank wall and the neighbour's boundary.

Two-storey and first-floor extensions — where the constraint moves from daylight to massing, roof form and the 45-degree impact on neighbouring windows. These almost always need full planning permission.

Wrap-around extensions — a rear and side extension combined. The best value per pound of construction, but the drawings have to resolve a complicated roof junction and often a new steel frame.

Porches, garage conversions and infills — smaller works that still need accurate drawings for building control, warranties and future sale.

What we produce

Every extension project runs through the same two-stage output. First a planning pack: existing and proposed floor plans, existing and proposed elevations, a roof plan, a site plan at 1:200 and a location plan at 1:1250, all correctly scaled, titled and north-pointed so your application validates first time.

Then, once the principle is agreed, a building regulations pack: dimensioned construction plans, sections through the new build-up, foundation and drainage information, insulation and U-value notes, ventilation and means of escape, structural openings marked for your engineer, and the specification notes a building control officer expects to see.

Permitted development or full planning?

A great many single-storey rear extensions fall under permitted development rights, which means no planning application — though most homeowners still want a Lawful Development Certificate as written proof, and conveyancing solicitors increasingly ask for one at sale.

The allowances depend on whether your house is detached or attached, how high the extension is, how close it comes to a boundary, and whether the property sits in a conservation area, a National Park or an Article 4 area. Flats and maisonettes have no permitted development rights at all, and rights are frequently removed on newer estates by planning condition.

We check the constraints before we design, so you are not paying to develop a scheme that was never going to work. Where permitted development is tight, we will often show you two options: one that stays inside the allowance, and one that goes further and needs an application.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to the questions we are asked most often.

What drawings do I need for a house extension?

For a planning application you normally need existing and proposed floor plans, existing and proposed elevations, a roof plan, a site or block plan at 1:200 and a location plan at 1:1250. If the work goes ahead you will then need a separate building regulations set with construction sections, dimensions and specification notes. We produce both.

How much do house extension drawings cost in the UK?

Fees vary with the size of the property, the complexity of the extension and whether you need planning drawings, building regulations drawings or both. We quote a fixed price for a defined scope after a short call, so you know the total before you commit — we do not work on open-ended hourly rates for residential drawing packages.

How long does it take to get extension drawings?

A typical single-storey extension planning set takes around one to two weeks from survey to issue, assuming prompt feedback. Building regulations drawings usually take a similar period afterwards. Two-storey and wrap-around projects take longer because the roof and structure need more resolution.

Do I need planning permission for a rear extension?

Not always. Many single-storey rear extensions are covered by permitted development rights, but the allowances are strict and are removed entirely for flats, and often restricted in conservation areas, on newer estates and under Article 4 directions. We check your property's constraints before designing so you know which route you are on.

Can you draw an extension if I already have an architect's sketch?

Yes. We regularly take a concept sketch, an estate agent's floor plan or a builder's idea and turn it into a properly surveyed, correctly scaled and fully annotated submission set. We will flag anything in the sketch that will not work in practice before we draw it up.

Do you deal with the council on my behalf?

We prepare and can submit the application, respond to validation queries and issue revised drawings if the case officer asks for amendments. For complex or contentious sites we will tell you honestly when a local planning consultant is worth the extra cost.
