Flat conversion drawings

House to flats, drawn for planning, acoustics and fire compartmentation.

Self-contained flat conversions where the layout, the sound insulation and the escape strategy are resolved together — because in a conversion they are the same problem.

Converting a house into self-contained flats is a fundamentally different exercise from converting it into an HMO. Each unit needs its own front door, its own kitchen and bathroom, its own services, and — critically — its own fire compartment and its own acoustic separation from the units above, below and beside it.

That last point is where conversions most often come unstuck. Approved Document E sets performance standards for sound insulation between dwellings, and existing timber floors in a Victorian or Edwardian house do not meet them without significant build-up. We design that build-up in at drawing stage, where it costs nothing to change, rather than discovering it during pre-completion testing.

Planning a flat conversion

Subdividing a house into flats is a material change of use and needs full planning permission — there is no permitted development route for a straightforward house-to-flats conversion. The application will be assessed on unit sizes, internal layout quality, daylight, refuse and cycle storage, parking pressure, private or shared amenity space, and the loss of family housing from the local stock.

Many councils apply the Nationally Described Space Standard, which sets minimum gross internal areas — commonly 37 square metres for a one-bedroom one-person flat and 50 square metres for a one-bedroom two-person flat, rising with bedroom and occupancy count. Some authorities adopt it formally as policy and some apply it as guidance, so we check before we plan the units.

There is a separate permitted development route for converting commercial premises to residential under Class MA, subject to prior approval and a set of qualifying conditions. It is a genuinely useful route for the right building, and a dead end for the wrong one — we will tell you which yours is.

Sound insulation and compartmentation

Between separate dwellings, Approved Document E requires floors and walls to achieve set airborne and impact sound performance, verified by pre-completion testing unless a Robust Details approach is used. In a conversion this usually means an independent ceiling below the existing joists, resilient layers and a heavy floating floor build-up above — which eats into floor-to-ceiling height and has to be planned around.

Fire compartmentation runs on parallel lines. Each flat becomes its own fire compartment, typically with 60 minutes of resistance to the common parts, fire-rated entrance doors with self-closers, a protected common stair, and an alarm strategy appropriate to the building's height and layout.

We draw the floor and wall build-ups in section, with the acoustic and fire performance annotated, so the contractor is pricing the right specification and building control can see the intent immediately.

Services, meters and the practical details

Every self-contained unit needs its own metered supply, its own consumer unit and its own boiler or heat source, plus independent drainage connections. Where those runs are going is a drawing question, not a site question — vertical service routes have to be found early because they dictate where bathrooms and kitchens can sit.

We coordinate the soil stack positions, meter cupboard locations, ventilation routes and communal areas on the plans, and mark structural openings for your engineer where walls are being removed or new openings formed.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to the questions we are asked most often.

Do I need planning permission to convert a house into flats?

Yes. Subdividing a single dwelling into self-contained flats is a material change of use and requires full planning permission in every case — there is no permitted development route for it. Converting certain commercial buildings to residential can be done under Class MA prior approval, which is a different and more limited process.

What are the sound insulation requirements between flats?

Approved Document E sets minimum airborne and impact sound performance for floors and walls separating dwellings, normally demonstrated through pre-completion testing or by following a Robust Details specification. Existing timber floors in period houses will not meet the standard unsupported, so an independent ceiling and floating floor build-up is usually required.

What is the minimum size for a flat in the UK?

The Nationally Described Space Standard sets minimum gross internal areas — commonly 37 square metres for a one-bedroom one-person flat and 50 square metres for a one-bedroom two-person flat, increasing with bedrooms and occupancy. Whether it applies as strict policy or guidance depends on your local authority's adopted plan, so we confirm it for your area.

Does each flat need its own utility meter?

For a genuinely self-contained unit, yes — separate metered supplies, individual consumer units and independent heating are the norm, and lenders and buyers will expect them. Planning the vertical service routes early is one of the main reasons to get proper drawings before starting on site.

Can I convert a commercial building into flats?

Often, through the Class MA permitted development route, which allows certain commercial, business and service premises to become dwellings subject to prior approval and qualifying conditions including vacancy periods and floor area limits. Not every building qualifies, and we check eligibility before taking on the design.

How long does a flat conversion drawing package take?

Planning drawings for a two- or three-unit conversion typically take two to three weeks from survey. The building regulations package, which carries the acoustic and fire detailing, usually takes a similar period and benefits from being started once the planning position is settled.
