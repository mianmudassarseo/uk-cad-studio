Drop kerb drawings

Drop kerb and vehicle crossover drawings for council applications.

Site plans, driveway layouts and visibility information prepared to your highways authority's requirements — including the planning application where the road is classified.

Get a free project reviewCall +92 305 6205832

A dropped kerb application is a small job with a surprisingly high refusal rate, and the reasons are almost always the same: the parking bay is too short, there is nowhere to turn so a car would have to reverse onto a busy road, visibility is obstructed, or there is a tree, lamp column, gully or utility box in the way that nobody checked.

We prepare the drawings that highways officers want to see, and just as usefully, we will tell you before you spend anything if the frontage you have is not going to work.

Two approvals, sometimes

Every vehicle crossover needs approval from the highways authority, because you are altering the public footway. That is a separate process from planning, run by the council's highways team, and it covers the technical construction of the crossing itself.

You will also need planning permission where the property fronts a classified road — an A, B or C road — or where the property is a flat or maisonette, is listed, or sits in a conservation area with restrictions. On an unclassified residential street with a standard house, planning permission is usually not required and the highways application alone will do.

There is a third requirement that catches people out: forming a driveway over five square metres normally requires permeable surfacing, or drainage directed to a permeable area within the property boundary, to avoid surface water discharging onto the highway. We specify this on the drawings.

What highways officers assess

Depth of the parking area. Most authorities require a minimum of around 4.8 to 5 metres from the back of the footway to any obstruction, so that a parked car sits entirely off the highway and does not overhang the pavement.

Visibility splays. Clear sightlines in both directions along the carriageway, unobstructed by walls, hedges or fences above a set height.

Street furniture and services. Lamp columns, trees, telecoms cabinets, drainage gullies, manholes and bus stops within the proposed crossing width. Relocating any of these is possible but adds significant cost, and the drawing needs to show them.

Turning provision. On busier or classified roads, officers often want to see that a vehicle can turn within the site and leave in forward gear rather than reversing out.

Proximity to junctions and crossings. Crossovers too close to a junction, a pedestrian crossing or a traffic calming feature are routinely refused.

What we draw

A scaled existing site plan showing the frontage as it is, including boundary treatments, levels, street furniture and existing surfacing. A proposed site plan showing the crossover width, the parking bay dimensions, the turning area where relevant, the surfacing specification and drainage arrangement.

Where planning permission is required, we produce the full application set including a location plan at 1:1250 and elevations of any boundary alterations such as removing a wall, gate or hedge.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to the questions we are asked most often.

Do I need planning permission for a dropped kerb?

You always need highways approval from the council, because you are altering the public footway. You additionally need planning permission if the property fronts a classified A, B or C road, or if it is a flat or maisonette, is listed, or sits in a conservation area where permitted development rights are restricted. On a standard house on an unclassified street, highways approval alone is usually sufficient.

How long does a drop kerb application take?

Highways applications commonly take around six to eight weeks to determine, though this varies significantly between authorities and some are considerably slower. If planning permission is also needed, allow a further eight weeks for that application, and note that the two can often run in parallel.

What size driveway do I need for a dropped kerb?

Most highways authorities require a minimum depth of around 4.8 to 5 metres from the back of the footway to any obstruction, so that a car can park entirely off the highway without overhanging the pavement. Some authorities require more, particularly where a turning area is expected, so we check the local standard before drawing.

Can I get a dropped kerb if there is a tree outside my house?

Sometimes, if the crossover can be positioned clear of the tree and its root protection area. Removing a street tree is rarely permitted and, where it is, the applicant normally bears the cost of removal and replacement planting. The drawing needs to show the tree position accurately so the officer can assess it.

Who builds the dropped kerb?

The construction of the crossing itself must be carried out either by the council's own contractor or by a contractor formally approved by the highways authority to work on the public highway. Work inside your boundary — the driveway surfacing and drainage — can be done by your own builder.

Do I need permeable paving for a new driveway?

For a new or replacement driveway over five square metres, yes in effect — you need either a permeable or porous surface, or a drainage arrangement that directs surface water to a permeable area within your own boundary. Laying an impermeable driveway that drains to the road normally requires planning permission.
