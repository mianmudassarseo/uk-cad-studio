Land Registry lease plans

Lease plans drawn to Land Registry requirements, first time.

Compliant plans for flats, commercial units and leasehold sales — correct scale, north point, clear demise and full floor level identification, so your application is not returned by a requisition.

A lease plan looks like the simplest drawing in the world right up until HM Land Registry returns it. Requisitions on lease plans are common and almost always avoidable: the scale is not stated, the plan is not to a recognised metric scale, the demise is ambiguous, the floor level is not identified, or a lift-and-shift of an estate agent's floor plan has been used without the necessary detail.

Every returned plan costs weeks. On a leasehold sale, weeks are exactly what nobody has. We produce lease plans that meet the Land Registry's published requirements so that registration proceeds without a query.

What Land Registry actually requires

The Land Registry's practice guidance is specific. A lease plan must be drawn to a stated metric scale — 1:100 or 1:200 is typical for a flat, 1:500 or 1:1250 for larger sites — and must be an accurate representation, not a sketch or an artist's impression.

It must show a north point, identify the floor level or levels the demise sits on, and clearly define the extent of the property being leased, conventionally edged in red. Where rights of way, parking spaces, bin stores, gardens or other rights are being granted or reserved, those must be shown and distinguished, usually by separate colours or hatching identified in a key.

It should also be capable of being identified on the Ordnance Survey map base — meaning the building has to be locatable, which is why a location plan is normally supplied alongside the detailed demise plan.

Common reasons plans get rejected

No scale, or a non-standard scale. "Not to scale" is an automatic problem. So is a plan reduced on a photocopier from A3 to A4 without the scale being corrected.

An estate agent's floor plan used as a lease plan. These are drawn for marketing, are frequently approximate, and rarely carry a north point, a stated scale or a clear demise boundary.

Ambiguous demise. If it is not obvious whether a balcony, a loft space, a hallway cupboard or a section of garden is inside or outside the red line, the Registry will ask.

Missing floor level identification. For flats in a multi-storey building, the plan must make clear which storey the demise occupies.

Colours that do not match the lease. If the lease text refers to an area edged blue and the plan shows it hatched green, that is a requisition.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to the questions we are asked most often.

What scale should a Land Registry lease plan be?

It must be drawn to a stated metric scale and be accurate. In practice 1:100 or 1:200 is normal for an individual flat, while 1:500 or 1:1250 suits larger sites and location plans. The scale must be stated on the drawing, and a plan marked "not to scale" will not be accepted.

Can I use an estate agent's floor plan as a lease plan?

Almost never. Marketing floor plans are approximate, typically lack a stated scale and north point, and do not clearly define the demise or the floor level. Using one is one of the most frequent causes of a Land Registry requisition.

What needs to be shown on a lease plan?

The extent of the demised property clearly defined and conventionally edged in red, a stated metric scale, a north point, identification of the floor level or levels, and any rights of way, parking spaces or shared areas being granted or reserved — shown in colours or hatching that match the wording in the lease.

How quickly can you produce a lease plan?

For a straightforward flat where we can access the property to survey, we can usually turn a compliant plan around within a few working days. Where accurate existing drawings are available, it can be faster. We understand that lease plans usually sit on the critical path of a sale.

Do I need a lease plan for a lease under seven years?

Leases of seven years or less are generally not registrable at the Land Registry, so a compliant registration plan may not be strictly required. A clear plan is still strongly advisable to define the demise and avoid disputes, and many landlords and lenders will expect one regardless.

Can you produce lease plans for commercial units?

Yes. We prepare lease plans for retail units, offices, industrial units and mixed-use buildings, including plans that show shared service areas, loading access, parking allocation and multi-floor demises.
