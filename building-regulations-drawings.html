Building regulations drawings

Building regulations drawings your contractor can actually price.

The technical package that sits behind the planning drawings — construction sections, U-values, ventilation, drainage and fire strategy, prepared for building control and for site.

Get a free project reviewCall +92 305 6205832

Planning permission decides whether you can build something. Building regulations decide whether what you build is safe, warm, ventilated and structurally sound. They are separate approvals with separate applications, and having one does not give you the other — a point that catches out a surprising number of homeowners each year.

Building regulations drawings are also the set your builder prices from. Vague drawings produce vague quotes, and vague quotes produce variations. A properly detailed package is the cheapest insurance available against a build that drifts twenty per cent over budget.

What the package covers

A complete building regulations set works through the Approved Documents that apply to your project. In practice that usually means: Part A structure, with openings and beam positions coordinated with your structural engineer; Part B fire safety, covering escape routes, alarm grades and fire-resisting construction; Part C site preparation and moisture resistance, including damp-proofing and floor build-ups.

Then Part F ventilation and Part L conservation of fuel and power, which together drive the insulation specification, U-value targets and mechanical ventilation strategy; Part K protection from falling, covering stairs, balustrades and guarding; Part M access, including level thresholds and WC provision; and Part H drainage, with above and below-ground layouts.

Recent additions matter too — Part O overheating for new dwellings and Part S for electric vehicle charging both apply to new build work and are increasingly picked up at plan check.

Full plans or a building notice?

There are two routes to building control approval for most domestic work. A building notice lets you start work with 48 hours' notice and no drawings, with everything inspected on site. It is quick, and it moves all the risk onto you and your builder: if an inspector rejects something at second fix, it comes out.

A full plans application submits drawings and specifications in advance for approval before work starts. It takes longer at the front end and costs more in fees, but you get a decision notice, your builder prices a defined specification, and there is a clear record of compliance for future sale.

We recommend full plans for anything structural, anything involving a change of use, and anything you will need to evidence at conveyancing. That is most projects. Building notice can be reasonable for straightforward like-for-like work.

Who checks it

You can use your local authority building control department or a private Approved Inspector — now regulated as Registered Building Control Approvers. Both can issue completion certificates. Private inspectors are often faster and more flexible on site visits; local authority building control is sometimes preferred for complex or contentious projects because they hold the enforcement powers.

We prepare packages for either route, and we will format and annotate the drawings to the conventions of whichever body you appoint.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to the questions we are asked most often.

What is the difference between planning permission and building regulations?

Planning permission governs whether a development is acceptable in principle — its use, appearance, scale and impact on neighbours. Building regulations govern how it is constructed: structure, fire safety, insulation, ventilation, drainage and accessibility. They are separate applications assessed by different people, and you can need both, either, or neither depending on the project.

Do I need building regulations drawings for an extension?

You need building regulations approval for virtually any extension. You do not strictly need drawings if you use the building notice route, but you will need them if you make a full plans application, and you will almost certainly want them anyway so that your builder is pricing a defined specification rather than guessing.

What is a full plans application?

It is the route where drawings and specifications are submitted to building control for approval before work starts. The plan checker reviews them, raises queries, and issues a decision. It takes longer upfront than a building notice but gives you approved drawings, comparable builder quotes and clear evidence of compliance for future sale.

How long does building control approval take?

A full plans application is normally determined within five weeks, extendable to two months with your agreement. Plan check queries are common on the first pass and are usually resolved quickly by issuing revised drawings, which we handle as part of the package.

Can I use a private building control inspector?

Yes. You can use either your local authority's building control department or a Registered Building Control Approver in the private sector. Both can inspect the work and issue a completion certificate. We prepare drawings suited to either route.

What happens if work was done without building regulations approval?

It can be a real problem at sale, since buyers' solicitors will ask for the completion certificate. Depending on the age of the work, options include a regularisation application to the local authority, which involves opening up work for inspection, or indemnity insurance in more limited cases. We can survey what was built and prepare drawings for a regularisation application.
