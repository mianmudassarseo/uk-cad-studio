Architectural design Liverpool

Architectural design and planning drawings in Liverpool.

Extensions, conversions and HMO schemes across Liverpool — a city of Georgian squares, Victorian terraces and one of the country's more actively regulated rental markets.

Get a free project reviewCall +92 305 6205832

Liverpool's residential stock runs from the Georgian terraces of the Canning area, through vast tracts of Victorian and Edwardian terraced housing, to interwar suburban semis further out. The city also has a large World Heritage-influenced conservation landscape in and around the centre.

For investors, Liverpool has long been one of the more attractive conversion markets in England, and the council has responded with active regulation of the private rented sector. That makes the licensing and planning position the first question on any conversion project here, not an afterthought.

Liverpool project types

Terrace extensions and loft conversions across Wavertree, Allerton and Aigburth are the core homeowner work. Many of Liverpool's Victorian terraces have good ceiling and ridge heights, which makes loft conversions viable more often than in cities with lower-built stock.

HMO and flat conversions are a substantial part of the market, particularly in the inner suburbs and around the universities. Liverpool has used Article 4 directions to control HMO conversion in parts of the city, and operates licensing with its own property standards.

Georgian and conservation area work in areas such as Canning and Falkner Square requires drawings that respond to sash window proportions, stucco and ironwork detail, and often listed building consent alongside planning permission.

FAQs

Liverpool planning questions

Local answers for property owners and developers in Liverpool.

Do I need planning permission for an HMO in Liverpool?

Article 4 directions restricting the permitted development right to convert a family home into a small HMO apply in parts of Liverpool, meaning a full planning application is required in those areas. Large HMOs of seven or more occupants always need permission. Coverage has changed over time, so we confirm the current position for your address.

Does Liverpool require a landlord licence?

Liverpool has operated extensive selective licensing of private rented property in addition to mandatory HMO licensing. Scheme boundaries and requirements change, so you should confirm the current position with the council — we design layouts to meet the property standards that apply to your licence type.

Can you prepare drawings for Georgian properties in Liverpool?

Yes. Georgian work needs drawings that get the proportions right — sash window subdivision, cornice and string course lines, railings and render detail. Where the building is listed we prepare drawings for listed building consent, and will advise if a heritage consultant should be involved.

What extensions are possible on a Liverpool Victorian terrace?

Single-storey rear extensions are the most common, often combined with a loft conversion. Many of these terraces have generous ridge heights that make dormer conversions viable. Plot depth and the 50 per cent curtilage rule usually set the limit, and we check conservation area status before designing.

Do you cover Merseyside as well as Liverpool?

Yes. We prepare drawings for projects across Sefton, Knowsley, St Helens and Wirral in addition to Liverpool City Council, checking each authority's own policies for the site.
