Garden rooms & outbuildings

Garden room and outbuilding drawings, with the rules checked first.

Garden offices, studios, annexes, garages and workshops — drawn against the permitted development limits that actually apply, with a Lawful Development Certificate where you want proof.

Garden rooms are sold as the easy project — no planning, delivered in a week, sign here. Frequently that is true. But the permitted development rules for outbuildings are more restrictive than most suppliers explain, and the two conditions that catch people out most often are height and use.

Height, because anything within two metres of a boundary is limited to 2.5 metres overall, which a pitched roof will exceed almost immediately. And use, because permitted development for outbuildings covers purposes incidental to the enjoyment of the dwelling — a home office or gym is generally fine, but a self-contained annexe with a bedroom, kitchen and bathroom that someone lives in independently is a separate dwelling, and that needs full planning permission.

The permitted development limits

Outbuildings are generally permitted development where they are single storey, sit to the rear of the principal elevation, cover no more than half the total area of land around the original house, and are not built as a separate dwelling.

Height is the binding constraint. Within two metres of any boundary, the maximum overall height is 2.5 metres. Beyond two metres from the boundary, you can go to 4 metres for a dual-pitched roof or 3 metres for any other roof form, with eaves limited to 2.5 metres.

The rights are restricted further in conservation areas, National Parks, Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty and the Broads, and are removed entirely for listed buildings, which need listed building consent. Flats have no permitted development rights for outbuildings at all. Many newer housing estates also have rights removed by planning condition, which is worth checking in your title documents.

Do outbuildings need building regulations?

Small detached outbuildings can be exempt, but the exemptions are narrower than people assume. A detached single-storey building under 30 square metres of floor area is generally exempt provided it contains no sleeping accommodation, and is either sited more than one metre from any boundary or constructed substantially of non-combustible material.

Cross any of those lines — over 30 square metres, someone sleeping in it, or within a metre of the boundary in combustible construction — and full building regulations approval applies, which brings in insulation, structure, electrical safety and fire requirements.

Electrical work in an outbuilding is notifiable under Part P regardless of whether the building itself is exempt, and needs to be carried out or certified by a competent person.

Annexes and independent living

An annexe for a family member is one of the most common reasons people come to us, and it is the area where the rules are least intuitive. If the accommodation is genuinely ancillary — used by a household member, sharing facilities and access with the main house — it may be acceptable as an extension of the existing dwelling.

If it functions as an independent household with its own entrance, kitchen, bathroom and no meaningful reliance on the main house, planning will treat it as a new dwelling. That triggers a full planning application, and often a condition tying the annexe to the main house so it cannot later be sold separately.

We design and draw both routes, and we are direct with clients about which one their brief actually falls into before any money is spent.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to the questions we are asked most often.

How big can I build a garden room without planning permission?

Under permitted development an outbuilding must be single storey, sit behind the principal elevation, and not cover more than half the land around the original house. Height is limited to 2.5 metres overall if it is within two metres of a boundary; beyond two metres you can reach 4 metres with a dual-pitched roof or 3 metres otherwise, with eaves no higher than 2.5 metres.

Do garden rooms need building regulations approval?

Not always. A detached single-storey outbuilding under 30 square metres is generally exempt provided it has no sleeping accommodation and is either more than one metre from any boundary or built substantially from non-combustible material. Exceed those limits and full building regulations approval is required. Electrical work is notifiable under Part P either way.

Can I sleep in my garden room?

Not under the outbuilding exemptions. Introducing sleeping accommodation removes the building regulations exemption and, if the space functions as independent living accommodation, it is likely to be treated as a new dwelling requiring full planning permission. An occasional guest room may be viewed differently from permanent occupation, but the distinction matters and is worth resolving before you build.

Do I need planning permission for a garden annexe?

It depends on whether the annexe is genuinely ancillary to the main house or functions as a separate dwelling. Ancillary accommodation used by a household member may be acceptable; a self-contained unit with its own entrance, kitchen and bathroom will normally require full planning permission, often with a condition preventing separate sale.

Should I get a Lawful Development Certificate for a garden room?

It is strongly worth it. An LDC is written confirmation from the council that the works are lawful, and it costs a fraction of a full planning application. Buyers' solicitors increasingly ask for evidence that outbuildings were lawfully erected, and without a certificate the burden of proof sits with you at sale.

Can I build a garden room in a conservation area?

Usually, but with tighter restrictions. In conservation areas, National Parks and AONBs, outbuildings to the side of the property are not permitted development, and total coverage allowances are reduced. Listed buildings need listed building consent regardless of size. We check the designation before designing.
