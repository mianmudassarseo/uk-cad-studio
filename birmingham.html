Architectural design Birmingham

Architectural design and planning drawings in Birmingham.

Extensions, conversions and HMO schemes across Birmingham — designed for the city's interwar semis, Victorian terraces and one of the country's most active conversion markets.

Birmingham is England's largest local planning authority by population, and its housing stock is unusually varied: dense Victorian terraces in the inner ring, vast interwar semi-detached suburbs, Bournville's model housing, and the very different character of Sutton Coldfield to the north.

That variety matters for drawings. An extension to a 1930s semi with a generous side plot is a completely different design problem from a rear extension on a tight inner-city terrace, and Birmingham's planning officers apply the city's design guidance with the specific character of each area in mind.

Common Birmingham project types

Side and rear extensions to interwar semis are the bread and butter of Birmingham residential work. Many of these houses sit on plots with real side space, which makes a two-storey side extension viable — though the council will look closely at whether it leaves an adequate gap to the boundary and avoids a terracing effect along the street.

Loft conversions on both terraces and semis are widespread. Hip-to-gable conversions are particularly common in Birmingham because so much of the interwar stock has hipped roofs, and squaring one off adds substantially more usable space than a dormer alone.

HMO conversions concentrate around the university areas and the inner suburbs. Birmingham has taken an active approach to managing HMO concentration through Article 4 directions, so the permitted development route for small HMOs is restricted — we confirm the current position for any address before advising.

Local planning context

What shapes a Birmingham application

The constraints we check before starting design work on any project in West Midlands.

Birmingham City Council

The Birmingham Development Plan and the council's residential design guidance govern extension scale, boundary gaps and amenity space.

Article 4 HMO restrictions

Birmingham operates Article 4 directions restricting C3 to C4 permitted development. We confirm the current coverage for your specific address.

Interwar semi character

Officers frequently require two-storey side extensions to be set back from the front elevation and set in from the boundary to preserve the rhythm of the street.

Conservation areas

Bournville, Moseley, Edgbaston and Jewellery Quarter areas carry conservation designations with tighter control on materials and alterations.

HMO licensing standards

Birmingham applies mandatory licensing with local amenity and room size standards that we design against directly.

Neighbouring authorities

Solihull, Sandwell, Dudley, Walsall and Wolverhampton are separate authorities with their own policies.

FAQs

Birmingham planning questions

Local answers for property owners and developers in Birmingham.

Do I need planning permission for a two-storey side extension in Birmingham?

Two-storey side extensions normally require full planning permission, and Birmingham City Council applies design guidance that typically expects the extension to be set back from the front elevation and set in from the side boundary to avoid a continuous terracing effect. We design to those expectations from the start.

Are HMOs restricted in Birmingham?

Birmingham uses Article 4 directions to remove permitted development rights for converting family homes into small HMOs in designated areas, meaning a full planning application is required. Applications are assessed partly on the existing concentration of HMOs nearby. We confirm the current position for your address before you commit.

Do you work across the wider West Midlands?

Yes. As well as Birmingham City Council we prepare drawings for projects in Solihull, Sandwell, Dudley, Walsall, Wolverhampton and Coventry. Each has its own local plan, which we check for the specific site.

What is a hip-to-gable loft conversion and do I need permission?

It squares off a sloping hip roof end into a vertical gable wall, creating substantially more usable loft space — very common on Birmingham's interwar semis. It is often permitted development within the volume allowances, but rights are restricted in conservation areas and on some estates, so we check before designing.

How much does an extension drawing package cost in Birmingham?

It depends on the size of the property and whether you need planning drawings, building regulations drawings or both. We provide a fixed quote for a clearly defined scope after a short call, so there are no open-ended hourly charges.
