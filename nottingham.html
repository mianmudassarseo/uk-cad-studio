Architectural design Nottingham

Architectural design and planning drawings in Nottingham.

Extensions, conversions and HMO schemes across Nottingham — a city where the HMO planning position needs checking before anything else, and where getting it wrong is expensive.

Get a free project reviewCall +92 305 6205832

Nottingham has one of the most actively managed HMO planning environments in England. With two large universities and a dense stock of Victorian terraces close to them, the city has used Article 4 directions extensively to control the concentration of shared housing.

For landlords and developers that changes the arithmetic entirely: the permitted development route from a family house to a small HMO cannot be assumed, and applications are assessed against the proportion of existing HMOs in the surrounding area. We check that position first, before any design work, because it determines whether a scheme is viable at all.

Nottingham project types

HMO conversions dominate the investor market here, concentrated in the terraced streets around the universities. Beyond the planning question, Nottingham operates licensing schemes with their own room size and amenity standards that are frequently more demanding than the national minimums.

Rear and side extensions to Victorian terraces and interwar semis make up most homeowner work. Nottingham's terraced plots are often reasonably deep, which gives more room for a rear extension than in denser cities, though the 50 per cent curtilage rule still applies.

Loft conversions are widespread across the two-storey terraces, with rear dormers the usual approach. The city also has a notable stock of properties with cellars, which are sometimes convertible subject to head height and damp.

What shapes a Nottingham application

The constraints we check before starting design work on any project in East Midlands.

Nottingham City Council

The council's local plan and residential design guidance govern extension scale, amenity space and conversion standards.

Article 4 HMO directions

Nottingham applies Article 4 directions restricting C3 to C4 permitted development across a wide area. We confirm the current coverage for your specific address.

HMO concentration policy

Applications are commonly assessed against the proportion of existing HMOs within a defined radius, which can be decisive.

Licensing standards

Selective and additional licensing schemes operate alongside mandatory licensing, with local amenity and room size requirements.

Conservation areas

The Park Estate, Sneinton and parts of the city centre carry conservation designations with tighter control.

Surrounding authorities

Rushcliffe, Broxtowe and Gedling are separate authorities with their own policies and different HMO positions.

FAQs

Nottingham planning questions

Local answers for property owners and developers in Nottingham.

Do I need planning permission for an HMO in Nottingham?

In areas covered by Article 4 directions — which extend widely across Nottingham — converting a family home into a small HMO for three to six people requires full planning permission rather than falling under permitted development. Large HMOs of seven or more always require permission. This is the first thing we check on any Nottingham HMO enquiry.

What is the HMO concentration policy in Nottingham?

The council commonly assesses HMO applications partly on the proportion of existing HMOs within a defined radius of the property, with the aim of preventing over-concentration in particular streets. Where the threshold is already exceeded, an application is significantly harder to win, which is why the check needs doing before you buy or design.

What are the minimum room sizes for a Nottingham HMO?

National mandatory licensing sets minimums of 6.51 square metres for one person over ten and 10.22 square metres for two, with space under 1.5 metres of head height excluded. Nottingham's licensing schemes may apply higher standards, so we design against the requirements published for your property's licence type.

Do you work in West Bridgford and Beeston as well as the city?

Yes. Those areas fall under Rushcliffe and Broxtowe respectively rather than Nottingham City Council, and they apply different policies — including different positions on HMO permitted development. We check which authority governs your address at the outset.

How long does a Nottingham planning application take?

Householder and minor applications have a statutory eight-week target. HMO change of use applications in Article 4 areas can attract objections and sometimes take longer, particularly where concentration is contested.
